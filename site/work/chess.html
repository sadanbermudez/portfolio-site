In plain words
AI chess coaches often invent tactics that are not on the board. Here a chess engine and the rules of chess supply every fact, and the language model only puts them into words. You can replay one of my games, try your own moves, or paste a game of yours.
Why it matters
The same problem appears wherever AI explains something: fluent text that is not true. Letting trusted tools own the facts, and checking each claim before it is said, is one way to stop it.
What I built
jarvis-chess, an MCP server with four tools: fetch recent games, review a game with Stockfish, analyse a position, and check a commentator's claims as true, false or uncheckable. On this page, Stockfish 19 also runs in your browser.
What I measured
The server reviews a 39-ply game in 6.5 s and checks 8 types of claim. The lighter engine in the browser finds 3 of the 6 blunders full Stockfish 19 finds on the game shown, so its counts are indicative.
Glossary: the technical words on this page
Stockfish
The strongest open-source chess engine. It scores positions and finds the best moves.
Ply
One move by one side. A 39-ply game has 39 single moves.
MCP
Model Context Protocol, an open standard for connecting AI models to tools. A server lists its tools; the model calls them.
Language model
The AI that writes the commentary. Here it narrates facts it is given; it does not decide them.
Claim check
A test of one statement, such as "the knight on e5 is hanging", against the board and the engine.
PGN
Portable Game Notation, the standard text format for a chess game.
Inaccuracy, mistake, blunder
A move that loses 0.1, 0.2 or 0.3 of the winning chances, on a −1 to 1 scale, as Lichess grades them.
ACPL
Average centipawn loss: how far a player's moves fell short of the engine's, in hundredths of a pawn.

How it works

4 tools

The engine supplies the facts, the model narrates

  • recent_games pulls my latest games from Lichess or Chess.com through their public APIs.
  • review_game runs Stockfish over every move, grades mistakes, and attaches rule-computed facts to each key moment: hanging pieces, pins, checks, mate in one, material.
  • analyse_position gives top engine lines for any position.
  • check_claims verifies what a commentator says before it is said: "the knight on e5 is hanging", "Ra8 is mate", "White is better". Each claim comes back true, false, or uncheckable.

The board's coach notes work the same way: each one names a pattern (fork, pin, skewer, hanging piece, mate) that the rules find on the board and the engine's line plays out. No pattern found, no claim; the note gives the evaluation instead.
