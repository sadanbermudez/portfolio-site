Chess commentary that cannot make things up

I am a FIDE Master, and I notice when an "AI coach" invents a tactic. So Jarvis reviews my games through a server where Stockfish and the rules of chess supply every fact, and the language model only narrates them.

Lichess blitz, 3+0. In a game this sharp both sides err; the review grades every move by the winning chances it gave away, using Lichess's method: on its −1 to 1 scale, a drop of 0.1 is an inaccuracy, 0.2 a mistake and 0.3 a blunder.

How it works

4 tools

The engine supplies the facts, the model narrates

  • recent_games pulls my latest games from Lichess or Chess.com through their public APIs.
  • review_game runs Stockfish over every move, grades mistakes, and attaches rule-computed facts to each key moment: hanging pieces, pins, checks, mate in one, material.
  • analyse_position gives top engine lines for any position.
  • check_claims verifies what a commentator says before it is said: "the knight on e5 is hanging", "Ra8 is mate", "White is better". Each claim comes back true, false, or uncheckable.

So I can ask Jarvis, by voice, to go through my last game, and every concrete statement it makes traces back to the engine or the rules.

Next: a faithfulness score. Have a local model comment on key moments freely, run every checkable claim through check_claims, and label the rest myself. The result is one number per model: the share of its chess statements that are true. Many projects pair an LLM with Stockfish; I have not found one that publishes that number.
