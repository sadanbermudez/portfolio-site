My story

Six cities, four fields, one chessboard

I have always loved science and technology. This is the route they took me on: biochemistry, photophysics, engineering and now AI, across Spain, Denmark, Scotland and Germany.

Scroll to follow the route

01 · MADRID · UNTIL 22

A chessboard at four, biochemistry at university

I grew up in Madrid and started playing chess when I was four. By sixteen I was a FIDE Master.

I studied a BSc in Biochemistry at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid (2010 to 2014), specialising in nanomedicine and molecular biology. My first paper came from that time: designed modular proteins used as scaffolds to stabilise fluorescent nanoclusters (Biomacromolecules, 2015).

02 · COPENHAGEN · TWO YEARS

Nanoscience, with a major in photophysics

A two-year MSc in Nanoscience at the University of Copenhagen, majoring in photophysics: how molecules absorb light and what they do with the energy. It is also where I wrote my first machine-learning code, in Python and R, for bioinformatics.

03 · GLASGOW · TWO YEARS

From scientist to engineer

I joined the Centre for Doctoral Training in Intelligent Sensing and Measurement, run by the universities of Glasgow and Edinburgh. It began in Glasgow, with a year of MSc in Engineering: engineering fundamentals applied to photonics.

04 · EDINBURGH · FOUR YEARS

A PhD in light, oxygen and fibres

My PhD detected singlet oxygen, the molecule that kills cells in photodynamic therapy, made by two-photon excitation inside hollow-core photonic crystal fibres. It took seven versions of the experiment to get there.

Along the way: a Leadership Award from the University of Edinburgh (2019), talks at the European Society for Photobiology, and the autumn academy of the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Light.

05 · WIESBADEN · TWO MONTHS

A short stop in Germany

Two months in Wiesbaden, between Scotland and Barcelona.

06 · BARCELONA · FOUR YEARS

Research, then industry, then AI

Two years as a postdoctoral researcher at IQS, Universitat Ramon Llull, on the EU project Light4Lungs: detecting singlet oxygen inside bacteria with photodiodes and photomultipliers.

Then two years at Sensofar, an optical metrology company, as Key Account Manager and Product Specialist for customers across the UK, Ireland, Italy, Poland, Australia and South-East Europe. There I designed an internal assistant on a large language model, with a Python document database behind it, to answer technical questions from our documentation faster. That was my first AI system built for real users.

07 · NOW

Building AI systems, and measuring them

Today I build AI systems the way I learned to build experiments: with a hypothesis, a control and a number at the end. A local AI assistant that runs on my laptop, an agent that operates a lab bench, a chess reviewer that cannot invent tactics.
