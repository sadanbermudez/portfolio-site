In plain words

What "next generation" means here

Today's chat models write one word-piece at a time, and each one waits for the last. Two ideas push past that. One is models that generate many words in parallel instead of one at a time. The other is running models far bigger than the computer's memory, by keeping the most-used parts in memory and reading the rest from the drive as they are needed.

Below, one of each, tested here: Stanford's next-concept model and two diffusion models, against chat models and an ordinary model; and a 284-billion-parameter model on a 64 GB laptop, then my own engine built to measure why it is slow.

Repo · inference performance

Why a 284B model runs at half a word per second

A storage benchmark, an AVX-512 kernel and profiled runs show the drive and the CPU mostly idle. Changing one variable at a time traced most of the gap to how the engine's threads wait on Windows. Reported upstream.

Follow one token through the model → Reproduced · parallel generation

A 25M model against the chat leaders

Stanford's next-concept model writes a whole answer coarse to fine in 12 parallel steps. On the same 50 hard Sudoku boards it solved 40 in half a second each on a laptop CPU; Claude Opus 5.5 solved 46, Sonnet 32, at about a minute each. Plus diffusion models, and why they need so many steps.
